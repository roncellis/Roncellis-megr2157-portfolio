## Objective

The objective of this assignment was to design a 3D-printable mount for a 24 V DC gear motor. The mount must connect the motor to rigid wall A, support an applied load of 300 N, maintain a factor of safety of 3, and limit the maximum free-end deflection to 0.30 mm.

The design was divided into two main structural features. Feature 1 supports and connects directly to the motor, while Feature 2 connects the mount to wall A and reinforces the overall structure.## Analyze


## FEATURE 1: MOTOR-ATTACHED PORTION

Analyze

Feature 1 is the horizontal portion of the mount that connects directly to and supports the motor. I treated this portion as a cantilever beam because it is supported on one end and carries the motor load near its free end. I identified the applied force, material properties, beam length, required factor of safety, and maximum allowable deflection before beginning my calculations.

I created a free-body diagram to show the applied load, support reactions, and maximum bending moment.

## FEATURE 2: WALL-ATTACHED PORTION

Analyze

Feature 2 is the vertical portion of the motor mount that connects the entire design to rigid wall A. This feature must support the force and bending moment transferred from the motor-attached portion. I treated this section as a supported structural member and identified the applied load, material properties, required factor of safety, allowable deflection, and distance between the motor and the wall.

## Decide

I used beam-bending stress and deflection calculations to determine the minimum dimensions required for Feature 1. I compared the thickness required to prevent yielding with the thickness required to meet the deflection limit. I selected the larger result and rounded the dimension upward to provide additional strength around the motor shaft opening and mounting holes.
